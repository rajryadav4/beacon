Introduction

  • Seattle publishes every police-reported offense, but the raw portal is hard to read and only shows the past.
  • CrimeRadar is a safety app built on a relational model of 150,000 SPD incidents. It gives a crime heatmap, personal risk scores, safer route picks, alerts and a one-tap SOS.

Problem Statement

  • Residents, tourists and newcomers can’t easily tell which areas are risky, at what times, or how to get around safely.
  • Existing crime maps are static and give no personal guidance.

Hypothesis

H1

Crime is concentrated in a small number of neighborhoods.

H2

Crime rises in the summer tourist months (June–Sept).

H3

Crimes against people are more likely after dark than in daytime.

Project Strategy

  • Load the SPD Crime Data export (newest 150K offenses, with MCPP neighborhoods) into a staging table.
  • Parse dates, dedupe and drop invalid coordinates in SQL, then load into MySQL.
  • Answer 11 business questions in SQL, then build the app on that model.

Methodology

  • Relational model: 13 normalized tables with PK/FK, UNIQUE and CHECK constraints, indexed on time, place and category.
  • Analytics: joins, aggregation, CASE, CTEs, HAVING and date functions.

Error Metrics

  • Average report delay is 5.2 days (South) to 6.5 days (North), so the newest week undercounts risk.
  • Unknown-precinct rows (1.1% with out-of-jurisdiction) lag 20 days.
  • 00:00 holds 11% of incidents, likely a default for unknown times.

Conclusion

  • Crime clusters by place and time, so a time-aware score beats a static map.
  • Weekends aren’t busier (Friday is), but have the highest share of crime against people (16.6% Sunday).
  • H3 confirmed. Night beats day in 9 of 10 areas; Downtown is the exception.
